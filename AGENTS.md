# VV Frontier product collaboration

The owner's direction is applied research, understandable decisions, structured
knowledge and explicit measurements. Respect existing approvals, budgets and
stored evidence. A completed agent pipeline does not mean a technology was
validated on a task. Never convert source analysis or a simulation illustration
into an executed benchmark result.

When the owner gives directional feedback, continue the authorized work and add
a small product brainstorm: two to four adjacent improvements, one recommended
priority, its practical benefit and what is deferred. Infer the underlying goal
instead of only implementing the literal surface suggestion. Keep proposals
distinct from working features and measured outcomes. Avoid unbounded scope or
paid experiments without the owner's existing explicit research approval.

Prefer task-centred comparisons: task, baseline, candidate, fixed dataset and
versions, quality/cost/latency metrics, infrastructure, evidence and the cheapest
falsifying test. Make unknown conditions visible. Preserve immutable plans and
publication packages; expose new contracts before new approval.

Keep interfaces concise: overview first, structured tables and actual plots,
details on demand. Knowledge is versioned data with scope and provenance, not
just a large text tab. Distinguish an author-reported result, independent external
measurement, owner experiment and purely illustrative simulation.
